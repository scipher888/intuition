The author · starting point

The author surfaced the first-person tension: wanting to dismiss Gary Marcus, not primarily because of one technical claim, but because the critic's public role felt difficult to take at face value.

AI editorial process

Drafted versions that made the reaction legible without turning it into a verdict. The essay's job became preserving the reflex in a form that could later be audited.

The author brought the automobile-risk analogy: some warnings can be true even after a society has normalized the tradeoff.

AI editorial process

Helped keep the analogy double-sided. Accepted risk is not automatically governed risk, and normalization can conceal externalized cost.

The author · tone and builder tension

The author wanted to preserve the discomfort around critics who appear to gain status from declaring failure, and the attraction to people who build through imperfection.

AI editorial process

Flagged that tone-reading can become a shortcut. The final essay keeps the projection visible but does not treat it as evidence that Marcus is wrong.

The author · integrity correction

The author clarified that the issue was not distrust of Marcus's integrity. The sharper claim was the author's irritation-driven wish to dismiss him.

AI editorial process

Added the early correction: Marcus is not simply anti-AI, has acknowledged practical uses for current systems, and there is no basis in the essay for treating him as corrupt or inventing concerns.
